## Overview

**Electrostatic charge dissipation** is one of the major concerns for satellites operating in the Earth's orbits. Under energetic plasma conditions, satellites may acquire very high negative potential (up to tens of kV) due to the collection of energetic plasma constituents — resulting in temporary outages and permanent damages to onboard equipment.

This research proposes and discusses physics-based schemes capable of mitigating/minimizing excessive charging effects over satellites under extreme plasma conditions in LEO/GEO environments.

## Key Results

- **Charge build-up estimation** on space objects based on charging dynamics as a function of ambient plasma parameters
- **UV illumination method**: Installing a suitable UV lamp on the satellite surface maintains the surface at a small positive potential (~2 V), preventing severe negative charging
- **Surface nanofabrication method**: Micro/nano-structuring of the surface fabric sustains the surface at a sufficiently low negative potential (~10 V)
- Both methods are shown to work efficiently in mitigating the potential threat of massive charging and safely performing satellite operations

## Motivation

Our calculations illustrate that in the absence of a significant charge dissipation mechanism, severe charging (tens of kV) occurs in the dark/shadowed regions at GEO and high latitude LEO. This can lead to:

- **Electrostatic discharge (ESD)** events causing electromagnetic interference
- **Permanent damage** to sensitive electronic components
- **Temporary outages** of satellite operations
- **Surface erosion** and degradation of materials
